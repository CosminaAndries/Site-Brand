The Story so Far

Early Career

1971 - 1980

Vivienne Westwood began her career in the fashion industry in the early 1970s.
In 1971, she opened a shop called "Let It Rock" with her then-partner Malcolm McLaren.The shop initially focused on Teddy Boy clothing and after a year nd a after a year, Vivienne’s interests had turned to biker clothing, zips, and leather. The shop re-branded with a skull and crossbones and was renamed Too Fast to Live, Too Young to Die.






Punk Era

Late 70's

Westwood and McLaren played a pivotal role in shaping the punk fashion movement. Their shop at 430 King's Road in London was renamed several times, reflecting the evolving styles and became eventually the "World Ends",with the iconic clock spinning backwards .The latter became a hub for the punk subculture.

Pirate Era

The 80's

In the early 1980s, Vivienne Westwood continued to evolve her design aesthetic. The "Pirate" collection (1981-1982) featured historical and romantic influences, and the brand embraced the New Romantic movement. During this time, Westwood's designs began gaining attention and recognition.



Harris Tweed and Orb Logo


Late 80's

The Harris Tweed collection (1987) marked Westwood's use of traditional Scottish fabric in her designs. Around the same time, the iconic Vivienne Westwood orb logo was introduced, symbolizing the brand's identity.

Worldwide Recognition

The 90's and beyond

Vivienne Westwood's designs gained international acclaim in the 1990s. Her runway shows became highly anticipated events, showcasing her innovative and sometimes provocative designs. The brand expanded globally, with flagship stores opening in various cities.



Activism and Sustainable Fashion

Vivienne Westwood has been an outspoken advocate for environmental and social issues.In addition to her contributions to fashion, she is known for promoting sustainable practices and raising awareness about climate change and social justice.




Damehood and Legacy

In 2006, Vivienne Westwood was awarded the Order of the British Empire (OBE) for her services to fashion. She was later promoted to Dame Commander of the Order of the British Empire (DBE)" in 2009. Westwood's legacy extends beyond fashion, as she continues to be a prominent figure in the industry and an advocate for sustainable and ethical fashion practices.


Vivienne Westwood's influence on fashion is profound, and her designs are celebrated for their creativity, boldness, and ability to challenge conventions. The brand remains a symbol of avant-garde fashion and conscious design.
